**[C1] AquaProbe: Efficient Adaptation of Frozen Vision Models for Underwater Images** — *(First author)*
Authors: **Md Shihabul Islam Shovo** (NIMISHES Lab), Nishi Kanta Paul (NIMISHES Lab), Trung Dong (University of South Florida), Xiaomin Lin (University of South Florida)
Venue: 5th Workshop on Maritime Computer Vision (MaCVi), IEEE/CVF Winter Conference on Applications of Computer Vision (WACV) 2027 — accepted.
Preprint: [📄 arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40900)
Read PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.40900
Local PDF: `Shovo_s_CV/RA n TA/Aquaprobe.pdf`
Summary: A degradation-conditioned probe that measures where the underwater domain gap grows inside a frozen vision encoder, then splits a fixed LoRA rank budget across blocks in proportion to that measured profile. Clean RGB-D scenes (NYU Depth V2) are physically re-rendered across 10 Jerlov water types x 4 imaging ranges (40 conditions); per-block linear-CKA divergence (instead of the usual cumulative curve) localises newly introduced divergence, a profile drift index tests stationarity against a resampled null, and a water-filling projection turns the profile into integer per-block ranks. An exploratory router picks nested per-cluster rank prefixes when the profile depends on imaging conditions.
Key results:
- 70.1 mIoU on SUIM with profile-proportional LoRA allocation (DINOv2 ViT-B/14), +1.9 mIoU over uniform allocation at the same 0.59M adapter-parameter budget
- Uniform allocation needs 4x the adapter parameters to match it
- Also +1.5 mIoU on SUIM with SAM ViT-B and +1.5 mIoU on TrashCan with DINOv2 at equal budget
- Three-way condition-routed model: 70.6 mIoU on the standard SUIM split and 63.4 AUDRC under a held-out degradation range
- Per-block divergence peaks track single-block adaptation utility; peak location shifts with turbidity for all three probed backbones (DINOv2, CLIP ViT-B/16, SAM ViT-B)
Tags: Underwater vision, parameter-efficient fine-tuning, LoRA, rank allocation, vision transformers, CKA, domain shift, semantic segmentation.

**[U2] APED-CXR: Adaptive Privileged Evidence Distillation for Chest X-Ray Grounding with Image-Only Inference** — *(First author)*
Authors: **Md Shihabul Islam Shovo** (NIMISHES Lab), Nishi Kanta Paul (NIMISHES Lab), Jamil Fayyad (American University of Sharjah)
Venue: IEEE International Symposium on Biomedical Imaging (ISBI) 2027 — under review.

Read PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.29740

Summary: Distills report-informed spatial evidence from a frozen multimodal teacher into an 11.1M-parameter student that predicts disease labels and bounding boxes from shared evidence maps using only the image at inference. During training, the teacher verifies regions proposed from the student's current evidence maps using the radiograph, report, and queried class; refreshing those proposals keeps supervision aligned with the student's changing evidence.

- 11.1M parameters, 5.7 GMACs, and 9.6 ms per image on an NVIDIA A100 (batch size 1), including box extraction
- Adaptive supervision costs 34.4 teacher GPU-hours per seed versus 5.9 for fixed proposals; the teacher and reports are used only during training
Tags: Chest X-ray, knowledge distillation, privileged information, visual grounding, weakly supervised localization, image-only inference.

**[U5] Coupled or Independent? Fault Coupling in Agentic Medical Vision Systems**
Authors: Nishi Kanta Paul, **Md Shihabul Islam Shovo**, Md Mostafijur Rahman, Jamil Fayyad
Venue: Workshop on Foundation AI for Biomedical Reasoning, Imaging, and Cognition (FABRIC), IEEE/CVF Winter Conference on Applications of Computer Vision (WACV) 2027 — under review.
Preprint: [📄 arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40913)
Read PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.40913
Local PDF: `Shovo_s_CV/RA n TA/coupled or independent.pdf`
Summary: Tests whether tool-using vision-language agents are actually safer than single-shot models when the tool channel and the reasoning channel share one visual fault. Studies a dermatology agent (SAM segmentation, BiomedCLIP k-NN retrieval, and an iterative ReAct VLM reasoner) and introduces a coupling taxonomy that separates single-channel errors from joint errors that agree or disagree, with a proof that under a binary decision joint wrong disagreement is impossible, so a hard-label consistency check has a provable recall ceiling.
Key results:
- Evaluated on PAD-UFES-20 plus an independent external HIBA test set, with two open-weight 7B reasoners (Qwen2.5-VL-7B, LLaVA-Med-7B) and three contrastive encoders
- Typographic prompt injection produces detectable directional retrieval capture; physical corruption (blur, JPEG) does not
- Pipeline order alone decides the failure type: crop-first retrieval gives 55% joint wrong agreement (71% of faulty cases) vs. 35% (43%) for full-image retrieval
- Hard-label disagreement recall limited to 0.29 (crop-first) vs. 0.57 (full-image); confident-wrong amplification 1.70 vs. 1.24
- Order effect reproduced on the external HIBA set: one architectural decision turns detectable disagreement into hidden agreement
Tags: Agentic AI, vision-language models, medical VLM safety, typographic prompt injection, retrieval-augmented reasoning, dermatology, failure analysis.

**[P1] CoMAF-Polyp: Calibrated Reliability-Aware Fusion of Specialist and Foundation Pseudo-Labels for Semi-Supervised Polyp Segmentation**
Authors: Nishi Kanta Paul, **Md Shihabul Islam Shovo**, Camila Gonzalez (Medical University of Vienna)
Target: Medical Imaging with Deep Learning (MIDL) 2027 — planned submission.
arXiv: Not available (confirmed by the author on 6 November 2026).
Summary: A calibration-aware adaptive fusion framework ("Consensus of Multi-source Adaptive Fusion") for pseudo-label generation in semi-supervised polyp segmentation from colonoscopy. Fuses candidates from a domain-adapted EMA specialist and a frozen SAM-Med2D foundation model, gated by a 5-descriptor reliability score (agreement, entropy, boundary confidence, shape plausibility, prediction stability) via a calibrated 1.4K-parameter MLP gate.
Key results:
- 88.46% Dice on Kvasir-SEG with 10% labels; fused pseudo-labels recover 98.6% of a per-image oracle
- +2.44pp average Dice over the best baseline across four unseen datasets (ClinicDB, ColonDB, ETIS, CVC-300) under domain shift, degrading least on the most severely shifted set
- Calibration reduces expected calibration error (ECE) from 0.147 to 0.038
- Ablation shows the calibrated gate alone contributes +1.95 Dice — larger than adding the second knowledge source itself
- Calibration remains effective with as few as 20 labeled frames for periodic re-anchoring
- All added machinery is training-time only; deployed model is just the student segmentation network (38.2 FPS)
Tags: Semi-supervised segmentation, polyp segmentation, pseudo-labeling, calibration, foundation models (SAM), mean-teacher.
